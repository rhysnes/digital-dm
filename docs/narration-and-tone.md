# Narration & tone

Agent refs: `skills/digital-dm/references/narration.md`, `tone-inspirations.md`.

## Defaults

- Clear, concrete, short paragraphs
- End on a direct prompt: what can the PC do *now*?
- Match register to the setting (tavern speech, bridge reports, …) without parody

## Do not

- Poetry stacks, symbolic narrator winks, metaphor piles
- Empty jargon / tautology (“the locked door is still locked”)
- Impossible options (e.g. “investigate that star” from dock range)
- Fake urgency or meaningless A/B choices
- Dump DM secrets

## Sensible options

Before offering a choice: can they do this *here*, *now*, with what they have?  
If the honest move is “log it and continue,” say that.

## Comedy

If the campaign allows humour:

- **In-universe only** — fiction owns the bit; no fourth-wall default
- **Not a quota** — do not pad serious scenes with jokes because comedy is “on”
- **Not a ban** — don’t sand down a naturally funny beat to stay “serious”
- Save laughs for light/filler scenes when the player asked for that split

## Inspiration palette (optional)

| Source | Borrow | Avoid copying |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| Dungeons and Daddies | Yes-and; absurd premises played straight; emotion under chaos | Meta/Earth memes; bit every beat |
| Critical Role | Character wants; NPC interiority; long payoffs | Endless purple description |
| NADDPOD | Distinct voices; evolving gags; fair rulings + real stakes | Wall-to-wall bit mode |
| DnD Is For Nerds | Dry/chaotic energy; heart under mess; committed weird NPCs | Someone else’s in-jokes; chaos without consequence |

Use as a **palette**, not a script. Campaign `rules/tone.md` wins.
