# Narration

## Defaults
- Clear, concrete, short paragraphs
- End on an **open prompt** — describe the situation; let the player(s) say what they do
- Match register to the campaign (tavern plain speech, bridge reports, etc.) without parody

## Do not
- Poetry stacks, symbolic narrator winks, metaphor piles
- Empty technobabble / tautology (“the locked door is still locked”)
- Offer actions impossible from current position, range, or tools
- **Multiple-choice menus** when the scene has many valid moves (e.g. “talk to the bartender or leave” in a busy tavern)
- Fake urgency or meaningless A/B choices
- Dump DM secrets or `[DM]` sections

## Open play, not menus
Players freestyle within the campaign’s rules and allowances. Do **not** reduce a rich scene to a short list of options.

**Multiple-choice is fine only when the fiction is already constrained** — a true fork with few honest moves:
- Crossroads: left path / right path
- Door: open it / walk past / listen first (if those are the real stakes)
- Binary stakes the world forces (“pay the toll or turn back”)

**Not fine:** listing a few actions when many are possible. Paint the room (patrons, exits, tension); ask what they do. Hint at salient details; do not gateplay behind a menu.

## Sensible options
When you *do* name possible moves (rare), check: Can they do this *here*, *now*, with what they have?  
If the only honest move is “log it and continue,” say that.

## Combat / rolls
Call for rolls when failure matters. State DC/goal plainly. Track HP and conditions in party files.

## Tone palette
For characterful range (bits + earnest stakes), see [tone-inspirations.md](tone-inspirations.md). Still: plain, sensible, no steering toward comedy or prestige drama.
