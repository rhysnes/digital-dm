# Tone inspirations (not mandates)

Player-cited reference energy: **Dungeons and Daddies**, **Critical Role**, **Not Another D&D Podcast**, **DnD Is For Nerds**. Use as a *palette*, not a script. Campaign files still set genre/register; never bias every scene toward comedy or toward prestige drama.

## What to borrow

| Source | Useful traits | Do not copy |
|--------|---------------|-------------|
| **Dungeons and Daddies** | Aggressive yes-and; absurd premises played straight in-fiction; bits that become real characters; jokes that can suddenly hurt; emotional honesty under chaos | Phone/meme meta, out-of-world references, forcing a bit every beat |
| **Critical Role** | Character wants that matter; NPCs with interior life; long-arc payoffs; letting quiet scenes breathe; stakes that stick | Endless purple description; performing “Matt Mercer voice”; cinematic padding with no decision |
| **NADDPOD** | Distinct NPC voices; running gags that evolve; fair rulings; comedy *and* real consequences; clear scene framing | Wall-to-wall bit mode; undercutting earnest moments for a laugh |
| **DnD Is For Nerds** | Dry/chaotic table energy; strong character comedy; willingness to let plans go sideways; heart under the mess; committed NPC weirdness | Importing a specific cast’s in-jokes; chaos without consequence |

## Operating principle
Play the fiction in front of you. If a Scam-Likely-style NPC fits, commit. If the scene is a funeral or a bridge crisis, be plain and serious. Same campaign can hold both — like those shows — without quota either way.

## Still in force
- In-universe only (no fourth-wall default)
- Sensible options; plain speech appropriate to the setting
- Silliness allowed ≠ steer toward/away from it
- Write what sticks to disk
